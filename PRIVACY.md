# Privacy

*Last updated 18 November 2026.*

Practice Vibes is made by one person, Camillo Patricelli. In short: **the app collects nothing about you and sends
nothing to the developer.**

## What the app does not do

- No accounts, no sign-in.
- No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting, advertising or tracking of any kind.
- No update checks or other automatic connections to the developer.

## Where your data lives

Everything you create (practice log, topics, notes, recordings, loops, settings) is stored on your device, in the
app's own folder and in the library folder **you** choose. If you put that folder in iCloud Drive, OneDrive, Google
Drive or similar, your cloud provider syncs it under its own terms; the developer never sees it.

When you share a topic, the app makes a file and hands it to the share method you pick (AirDrop, Mail, a chat app...).
Private notes are never included. What happens to the file then is between you and the people and services you send
it to.

## When the app connects to the internet

Only when you ask it to:

- **YouTube videos.** The embedded player page is loaded from GitHub Pages
  (camillopatricelli.github.io) and plays the video from YouTube. Your device then talks directly to GitHub and to
  Google/YouTube, which see your IP address and apply their own privacy policies
  ([GitHub](https://docs.github.com/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement),
  [Google](https://policies.google.com/privacy)).
- **Stem separation** (Windows, macOS). The first time you use it, the app downloads the separation model from this
  project's GitHub releases. GitHub sees your IP address. The separation itself runs entirely on your computer.
- **Links and web pages** you add to topics or to Links open in the app's viewer or in your browser, which connect
  to those websites as usual.

## Plugins

Effect and instrument plugins (AUv3, Audio Units, VST3) that you load are made by other companies and run their own
code inside the app. What they do, including any network access, is outside the developer's control: check their own
privacy policies.
