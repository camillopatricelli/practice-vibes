# Audio setup: what to plug in

Practice Vibes listens to your instrument: to log your practice, tune, loop and record. What you plug in makes the
difference between "it just works" and "I keep pressing Start".

---

## The best: an audio interface (sound card)

A small USB **audio interface** with an instrument input (often called Hi-Z or "Inst"). Guitar or bass straight into
it, headphones into its headphone socket. On the iPad, use one that works with iPad (USB-C, or an iRig-type adapter).

Why it's best:
- **The practice log is fully automatic.** The app only hears your instrument, so it counts exactly the minutes you
  play and pauses when you stop.
- **Low delay** between playing and hearing yourself (chapter 2 explains how to get it down).
- Clean sound for effects plugins, loops and takes.

Keyboards: the keyboard's line or headphone output into a line input. Singers: a microphone into the interface's mic
input (a close mic hears much less of the room than a built-in one).

**On Windows**, install the interface's **ASIO** driver from its maker's website if it has one, and pick **ASIO** in
Audio settings: it gives the lowest delay.

---

## A MIDI keyboard counts as playing

Connect a MIDI keyboard (USB, or Bluetooth MIDI) and the **notes you play count as practice time**, like sound on an
audio input. You don't need an audio input for it. To hear the keyboard through the app, add an instrument plugin in
the Rig (chapter 10); or just listen to the keyboard's own speakers.

If other MIDI gear sends notes too (a pad, a sequencer), choose the one device that counts: **Audio settings** ›
**MIDI notes** › **Only from**. A footswitch's presses never count.

---

## Not recommended: the device's built-in microphone

The microphone in an iPad or laptop hears the whole room: talking, the TV, the street. If the app counted that, your
log would fill up with noise. So with a microphone the app doesn't count by itself: you tap **Start** and **Pause** on
the Today tab (after 10 minutes of silence it pauses by itself).

It's fine for trying the app, for the tuner and for an acoustic instrument with nothing else around. But for the
"just play" experience, use an interface or a MIDI keyboard.

The app guesses which kind of input you have. You can change it per input in **Audio settings** › **Practice log**.

---

## Headphones

Use **wired** headphones. **Bluetooth headphones** add a delay (often 150 ms or more) that no setting can remove: fine
for listening to songs, not for hearing yourself play.

If you hear your instrument through the app on speakers while a microphone is the input, you get feedback (howling).
That's why the app always starts with your instrument muted: turn on **Rig** › **Monitor input** when you're on
headphones.
