Voice leading

Each chord is the voicing on the chosen string set that moves least from the one before, starting in the selected position. A solid ring marks a note held over from the previous chord. Uses the triads or 7th chords chosen above.

Quiz

Random voicings from the current scale and key, in any position. Name the chord, the inversion, or the job of the ringed note.

Reading the boards. High e is on top, low E at the bottom, as in tab. Columns are frets relative to n; pick a key to see real fret numbers. In triad and 7th view the board shows a fret either side of the shape, because some voicings borrow a note from next door: those have a dashed ring.

Positions. The five CAGED boxes are measured from the scale's root. For minor-type scales the same boxes hold the minor versions of the shapes (Em, Am and so on). The seven 3-notes-per-string patterns each start on a different scale degree on the low E. Positions below the nut are shown 12 frets up.

Voicings. Triads are close-voiced, one note per string on three adjacent strings. Drop 2: take a close seventh chord and drop its second-highest note an octave, giving four adjacent strings. Drop 3: drop the third-highest note instead, which skips a string (shown with a dot, e.g. e B G · A).

Whole neck. Choose Neck as the position to see every voicing of a chord on one string set, linked up the fretboard. On a three-string set a triad cycles through its three inversions; a seventh chord through four.

Modes. A mode uses the major scale's notes with a different home note. Dorian is the major scale from its 2nd degree, Mixolydian from its 5th, and so on. The page renumbers everything from the mode's own root, so the chord qualities shift: Dorian's IV is major, Mixolydian's v is minor.

Sound. Play uses a simple synth. With no key chosen it plays from A.
